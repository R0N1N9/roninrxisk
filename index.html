01 · Overview

What is PSSD?

Post-SSRI sexual dysfunction is a condition in which sexual side effects of serotonin reuptake inhibitor antidepressants (SSRIs and SNRIs) persist — sometimes for years — after the drug has been stopped. It is most commonly characterised by genital numbness, weak or pleasureless orgasm, loss of libido and erectile dysfunction. Many people also report non-sexual symptoms such as emotional blunting.

  • Genital numbness / reduced sensation
  • Weak, muted or pleasureless orgasm
  • Loss of libido
  • Erectile or arousal difficulties
  • Emotional blunting
  • Cognitive changes reported by some patients
Related conditions

PFS & PRSD

Similar enduring syndromes are reported after finasteride (post-finasteride syndrome) and isotretinoin (post-retinoid sexual dysfunction). The 2022 diagnostic criteria cover all three, plus PGAD after SRIs.

Open question

How common is it?

Nobody knows yet. Prevalence studies are hard to design, many patients never connect symptoms to a past prescription, and no evidence-based treatment has been established.

Why RoninRxISK

A disclosure problem

A harm that's under-reported, slow to be acknowledged and hard to measure looks a lot like an unpatched vulnerability. RoninRxISK applies the RoninSec mindset: document, verify, disclose.

02 · Disclosure timeline

From forum reports to regulator labels

1990s–early 2000s

Patient reports

Accounts of lasting sexual side effects circulate in online communities, where the term "PSSD" emerges.

2006

First in the medical literature

Case reports by Bahrick and by Csoka & Shipko describe persistent dysfunction after stopping SSRIs.

2011

US fluoxetine label

US product information for fluoxetine adds a warning that sexual side effects may persist after discontinuation.

2012 onward

Lareb reports

The Netherlands Pharmacovigilance Centre Lareb begins publishing reports on the condition.

2013

DSM-5 acknowledgement

DSM-5 notes that SRI-induced sexual dysfunction may, in some cases, persist after the drug is stopped.

2019

European Medicines Agency

Following a petition with named patient reports, the EMA asks for SSRI/SNRI labels to warn of long-lasting sexual dysfunction after discontinuation.

2022

Diagnostic criteria published

A multidisciplinary panel led by David Healy publishes criteria for PSSD, PFS, PRSD and post-SRI PGAD in the International Journal of Risk & Safety in Medicine.

2025–2026

Funded research & regulator review

Dedicated trainee research grants open in Canada, an Australian biological study is announced, and New Zealand's Medicines Adverse Reactions Committee reviews PSSD again in June 2026.
